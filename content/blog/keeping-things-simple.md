## Simplicity is an active choice

It is easy to add another section, another control or another animation. Removing something takes more confidence because the absence is visible.

For me, simplicity starts with the question: what is the visitor trying to do here? Once that answer is clear, everything else has to earn its place. A quieter interface is often more generous because it leaves room for the important things to be noticed.

## Reduce the decisions

Good interfaces do not make people decode the layout before they can use it. Clear headings, predictable actions and consistent spacing reduce the number of decisions a person has to make.

This is especially important on smaller screens. A design that only works when there is plenty of space is not simple; it is unfinished. Responsive work is the process of deciding what deserves to stay visible at every size.

## Leave the useful parts

Simplicity is not minimalism for its own sake. It is a way to protect the purpose of a page. Keep the character, keep the useful details and remove the friction around them.
