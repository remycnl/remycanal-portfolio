## A portfolio should feel like a place

This portfolio started with a simple question: how can I show the way I work, rather than only showing the work itself? A grid of thumbnails is useful, but it rarely says anything about the person who made it.

I wanted this site to feel closer to a small studio than a catalogue. The typography, the pauses between sections, the movement and the little unexpected details all have a job: they create a rhythm for exploring the projects.

## Designing the system

Nuxt gives me a strong foundation for that kind of work. Content stays close to the code, pages remain fast and the components can stay focused. I can spend time on the experience without turning the project into a pile of one-off effects.

The most important decision was to keep the visual rules small: a limited colour palette, a few type styles and motion that responds to the way a visitor is moving through the page. Constraints make the interesting decisions easier to see.

## The details are the work

The final pass is where the portfolio began to feel like mine. Loading states, focus styles, responsive spacing and reduced-motion behaviour are not glamorous features, but they are what make an interface feel considered.

There is still plenty to refine. That is part of the point: a portfolio is never finished, it is a living record of how I think and build.
