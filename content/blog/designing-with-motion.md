## Motion gives time a shape

A page is not only a collection of positions. It is also a sequence. Motion helps a visitor understand what changed, what matters and where to look next.

That does not mean everything should move. When every element calls for attention, the interface becomes noisy. I prefer a small number of clear gestures: an entrance that establishes hierarchy, a transition that preserves context and a response that confirms an action.

## The three questions I ask

First: what relationship does this movement explain? If the answer is none, it probably does not need to exist.

Second: can the same idea work at a slower speed? Slower motion often feels more deliberate and gives the content time to arrive.

Finally: what happens when motion is reduced or unavailable? A good interaction should remain understandable without its animation. Reduced motion is not a degraded version of the experience; it is another valid presentation of it.

## Make it feel inevitable

The best transitions are almost invisible. They do not show off the implementation, they make the next state feel like a natural continuation of the previous one.

That is the standard I aim for: movement with a reason, a comfortable pace and enough restraint to let the content stay in charge.
