In June 2023, a notary office in Lyon moved from the Brotteaux district to the quai Joseph Gillet. It took the opportunity to start over: new address, new name (it was called Brotteaux Lafayette Notaires), and a new image. I worked on the identity and the website.

![The homepage](/works/officedeslumieres/overview.webp "Homepage")

## The brief

A notary office has to look serious and sound reassuring. It also has to look like a place you can walk into without feeling lost. The identity and the site had to say "expert" and "approachable" together, and to feel contemporary for an office whose history goes back to 1780.

## Approach

The name is a play on light, and so is the tagline: *Notaires éclairants pour clients éclairés* ("enlightening notaries for enlightened clients"). The visual identity follows the idea. A warm yellow stands for light, and arches and soft shapes, drawn as SVG, bring in an architectural feeling without being heavy.

## The pages

**Home.** The tagline, a short paragraph about the move and the new name, the three areas of expertise, and a few key figures: five notaries, twenty years of experience on average, 99% of deeds signed electronically, and 1780 as the founding date. Having 1780 and "electronic deeds" in the same row says in one glance what the office wants to be.

**Office.** Who they are and how the team works.

**Expertises.** One page each for real estate law, family law and company law, so each client can go straight to their own situation.

**Useful documents.** Clients can prepare their appointment by checking the documents to bring. It saves time on both sides, and it is the page that really helps people.

**News, fees and contact.** A news section for legal updates, a page with the fees, and a contact page. A link to the client space leads to a secure external platform.

![Browsing the site](/works/officedeslumieres/demo.mp4 "Homepage, expertises, useful documents")

## Development

The front end is built with Nuxt, and the content (news, documents, expertise pages) is managed in a headless CMS, so the office can publish without calling a developer.
