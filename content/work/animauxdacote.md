Animaux d'à côté is a platform built around the well-being of pets. Think of it as a Doctolib for animals, with a lost-pet network and a neighborhood side to it. I led the art direction, the interface and the development.

## The brief

Taking care of a pet involves many small tasks spread across different places: finding a vet, booking an appointment and, in the worst case, searching for a lost animal. The app brings these together and adds something that is usually missing: the people living nearby.

![Overview of the app](/works/animauxdacote/overview.webp "The app, in a few screens")

## Three needs, three tones

The app has to be reassuring when you book a check-up, quick and clear when your pet has gone missing, and friendly when you talk to neighbors. That is a wide range for one interface, so the art direction is built to hold all three.

The coral red is warm and close to a human tone, and it is also strong enough to signal urgency. Rounded shapes and friendly illustrations soften the medical side. Spacing and typography stay calm and legible, so the app is still easy to use in a stressful moment.

## Booking care

The booking flow is deliberately short: find a professional nearby, see availability, pick a slot, confirm. The pet's health information is stored once and reused, so nobody fills in the same form twice.

![Booking an appointment](/works/animauxdacote/booking.mp4 "From search to confirmation")

## Finding a lost pet

This is where speed matters most. Declaring a lost animal takes a few fields and a photo, and the alert reaches people in the area right away. The page for a missing pet puts the photo, the last known location and the contact button above everything else.

::media{src="/works/animauxdacote/lost-pet.webp" alt="Lost pet page" caption="Lost pet page: photo, location, contact. Nothing else."}
::

## The neighborhood

The social side is made for helping each other: sharing tips, looking after a pet when someone is away, and spreading the word when an animal goes missing. I kept it light and local, with no feed to scroll for hours.
